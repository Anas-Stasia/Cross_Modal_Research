# Ethics Statement

## Project
This project studies whether disagreement between text, audio, and visual content in TikTok videos is associated with observed engagement. It uses precomputed embeddings and engagement metadata from a dataset described as a teaching/proof-of-concept resource.

## Privacy and data protection
Social media content may contain identifiable people, voices, usernames, locations, or sensitive context. Use the dataset only under its terms and applicable institutional requirements. Do not re-identify creators or link records to external personal information. Avoid publishing raw records or identifiers unless permitted and ethically justified, secure the data, and restrict access appropriately.

The paper does not document formal ethics review, consent, or institutional approval. This statement does not claim approval was obtained. Confirm University of Ghana requirements and dataset conditions before further use or publication.

## Risks and misuse
The score could be misused to judge individual creators, infer intent, or support unsupported causal claims. Do not use it for creator ranking, punitive moderation, employment, financial, or other consequential decisions.

## Bias and representativeness
The sample covers five days of Explore-page content and may not represent all TikTok uploads. Popularity, category, distribution, video age, and scrape timing can affect engagement. The supplied unimodal embeddings may not faithfully measure semantic relations across modalities.

## Reporting
Report null and weak results, limitations, and effect sizes. Do not claim divergence causes engagement or that this analysis explains TikTok's recommendation algorithm. Statistical significance should not be confused with practical significance.

## Compute
The paper does not report hardware, run time, or energy use. A reproduction should document compute resources where feasible.
